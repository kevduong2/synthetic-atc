# Radio/VHF Narrowband Channel Simulation for Speech Data Augmentation — Literature Survey (as of 2026)

## 1. Physics/DSP of the VHF AM aviation channel: what is documented

**Bandwidth and modulation.** The regulatory anchor is ICAO Annex 10 (Aeronautical Telecommunications), which specifies DSB-AM voice on VHF (118–137 MHz) with audio limited to roughly **300–2500 Hz** for intelligible voice (some emission specs allow up to 2700 Hz); 8.33 kHz channel spacing tightened these limits further ([ICAO Annex 10 Vol. II/V overview](https://talkiewaves.com/icao-annex-10-volume-ii-vhf-communication/), [ICAO Doc 9718](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/FSMP/Doc.9718-Vol-I-AdvanceUneditedVersion-2021.pdf)). This is the single most load-bearing physical fact for simulation: real ATC audio has essentially no energy below ~300 Hz or above ~2.5–3.4 kHz, and corpora reflect it — LDC-ATCC is distributed at 8 kHz/16-bit, recorded "without squelch or silence elimination" (noted in ATCO2 lessons-learned paper, footnote 12).

**Measured channel characterization.** The best empirical characterization of live VHF ATC audio comes from the ATCO2 project ([Zuluaga-Gomez et al., "Lessons Learned in ATCO2: 5000 hours of ATC Communications," arXiv:2305.01155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01155); [corpus paper arXiv:2211.04054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04054)). Their pipeline captures raw I/Q via RTLSDR-Airband receivers, demodulates with CSDR, and estimates per-segment SNR clamped to a **0–40 dB range**; segments below 0 dB SNR are discarded as unintelligible, and a quality score combining avgSNR, speaker count, speech ratio, English-language score, and word confidence ranks recordings. Qualitative finding: "the majority of data is clean, but there are some very noisy segments," with accent variability at least as big a problem as noise. They report WERs of ~17.9%/24.9% on their 1h/4h test sets with models trained on 2500 h of pseudo-labeled VHF data — versus far worse for out-of-domain models — quantifying the size of the ATC channel+domain gap.

**Individual artifact modeling (AGC, squelch, heterodynes, co-channel interference).** No peer-reviewed measurement study was found publishing parameter distributions for ATC squelch tails, heterodyne whistles, PTT clicks, or AGC dynamics. What exists: engineering/patent descriptions of AGC-based and noise-operated squelch ([US3350650](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3350650), [repeater-builder squelch notes](https://www.repeater-builder.com/micor/micor-bi-level-squelch-circuit.html)), and non-academic DSP simulators that model reception physically — e.g., the EffeTune lo-fi plugin suite has a shortwave-radio simulator with frequency-selective fading, co-channel heterodyne whistle, IF filtering, and AGC-coupled noise ([Frieve-A/effetune docs](https://github.com/Frieve-A/effetune/blob/main/docs/plugins/lofi.md)). **Uncertainty flag:** academic work stops at "filters + tanh distortion"; realistic squelch/heterodyne parameters must be measured from LiveATC/ATCO2 recordings or borrowed from radio-engineering practice, not cited from a paper.

**ATC-specific channel simulation in ASR papers.**
- [Bagat et al., "Synthetic Audio Generation Framework for ATC Speech Recognition" (arXiv:2606.21340, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21340) — their "ATC Acoustic Simulation" (AAS) is deliberately minimal: **downsample to 8 kHz and back to 16 kHz, high-pass at 200 Hz, then mix in real background noise separated from a paired real recording**. Despite its simplicity, AAS cut TTS-synthetic-only fine-tuning WER from **53.88% to 33.77% (37% relative)** on ATCO2 with Whisper-small. Real+synthetic fine-tuning reached 21.64% vs 22.69% real-only. Headline: "matching the physical acoustic environment... consistently benefits ASR."
- [Wee et al., "Adapting ASR for Accented ATC Communications" (arXiv:2502.20311, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20311v1) — simulate radio transmission with **high/low/band-pass filters plus hyperbolic-tangent distortion** (from audiomentations), tanh as a model of receiver nonlinearity. Results mixed: augmentation helped Whisper-small (0.122 val WER) but Whisper Large v3 Turbo did better *without* it (0.118) — a caution that heavy channel augmentation can hurt large pre-trained models. Final test WER 9.82% on SEA-accented ATC.
- A Research Square preprint on data-efficient American-English ATC fine-tuning uses a **300–3400 Hz Butterworth bandpass + EBU R128 loudness normalization + five-fold stochastic augmentation**, reporting 30.3%→13.7% WER from ~15 min of labels ([rs-8970162](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-8970162/v1); paywalled — chain parameters unverifiable). A widely used community model (Whisper-medium.en fine-tuned on ATCO2+UWB-ATCC with Gaussian noise + pitch-shift augmentation) reports 94.6%→15.1% WER, 84% relative ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/jacktol/whisper-medium.en-fine-tuned-for-ATC-faster-whisper)) — mostly domain/phraseology adaptation rather than channel simulation per se.

**Real (not simulated) channel degradation at scale.** The DARPA RATS collection is the reference for what real radio channels do to speech: LDC **retransmitted ~400 h of source telephone speech over eight physical transceiver configurations simultaneously** (~3100 h total) for SAD/LID/SID/KWS on severely degraded radio audio ([RATS collection paper, LREC 2014](https://aclanthology.org/L14-1089/); [LDC2017S20](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2017S20)). That DARPA paid for physical retransmission rather than simulation is itself evidence of how hard faithful radio-channel simulation was judged circa 2012 — and no modern paper found rigorously quantifies sim-vs-retransmission for ASR (**gap worth noting**).

## 2. Toolkits for telephony/radio-style channel augmentation

- **[audiomentations](https://iver56.github.io/audiomentations/)** (and GPU sibling [torch-audiomentations](https://github.com/iver56/torch-audiomentations)) covers nearly the whole radio chain: BandPass/HighPass/LowPass/BandStopFilter, Mp3Compression, ClippingDistortion, TanhDistortion, BitCrush, Aliasing, AirAbsorption, ApplyImpulseResponse, RoomSimulator, AddShortNoises (transient interference), Gain/GainTransition (crude AGC/fading proxy), Limiter. The ATC papers above draw directly from it.
- **[WavAugment](https://github.com/facebookresearch/WavAugment)** (Kharitonov et al., IEEE SLT 2021, [arXiv:2007.00991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00991)): time-domain pitch/reverb/additive-noise/time-dropout/band-reject/clipping; pitch+noise+reverb gave **18–22% relative** improvement for CPC representation learning.
- **Kaldi/ESPnet-style room-sim + noise (MTR)**: random RIR convolution plus MUSAN-style noise mixing; Google's production formulation samples from **3M pre-generated room configs, 0–4 noise sources, SNR 0–30 dB, RT60 0–900 ms, mic distance 1–10 m** ([Narayanan et al., "Toward domain-invariant speech recognition via large scale training," SLT 2018, arXiv:1808.05312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05312)). ATCO2's Kaldi recipe used standard 3-fold speed perturbation (190 h → 573 h).
- **Standards-grade telephony tools**: ITU-T G.191 Software Tool Library provides reference codecs, band-limiting filters (IRS/MSIN), and error insertion ([openitu/STL](https://github.com/openitu/STL)); **FaNT** (Filtering and Noise Adding Tool, Hirsch) is the tool behind Aurora-2/Aurora-4 noisy-telephony benchmarks, adding noise at controlled SNRs after ITU channel filtering ([FaNT source](https://github.com/i3thuan5/FaNT), [Aurora-2 usage](http://www.seas.ucla.edu/spapl/paper/zhu_eurospeech01.pdf)). Closest thing to "NIST-style" reproducible channel simulation.
- **SpecAugment** ([Park et al., Interspeech 2019, arXiv:1904.08779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08779)) remains the standard feature-domain complement, orthogonal to waveform channel simulation; ATC papers routinely stack it on top.

## 3. Codec simulation and low-bitrate streaming (LiveATC-style)

The strongest quantitative result is Google's MTR study ([arXiv:1808.05312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05312)): training sampled uniformly from **7 codec conditions — MP3 at 128/32/23 kbps, AAC at 128/64/23 kbps, and no codec (constant-bitrate FFmpeg)** — plus 8 kHz downsampling at p=0.5. On Voicesearch: without codec training, WER rose 10.5 (clean) → 13.6 (MP3 23k); with codec simulation it recovered to **10.6 on MP3 23k (~20% relative gain)** and generalized to **unseen codecs** (Opus 24k: 10.8→10.2; SBC: 10.7→10.2). Noise MTR simultaneously improved noisy Dictation WER 30.6→12.3 (~60% relative) with no clean-condition penalty. This is the key citation for "codec augmentation transfers across codecs, so exact LiveATC codec matching is non-critical."

Supporting: Opus at 6 kb/s costs roughly **+1 pp WER**, about half recoverable with codec-enhancement post-filters ([NoLACE, Interspeech 2023, arXiv:2309.14521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14521)); Amazon studied Opus bitrate allocation for far-field ASR under fixed budgets ([arXiv:2106.07994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.07994)). **Uncertainty flags:** claims that MP3 ≤64 kbps adds >10% relative WER come from a practitioner blog ([team-connect](https://team-connect.co.uk/info/audio-codecs-explained)), not peer review. No citable measurement of LiveATC's actual stream configuration was found (community knowledge says mono MP3 ~16–24 kbps, unsourced); ATCO2 ships MP3-transcoded audio and notes LiveATC-sourced audio is generally lower quality than their own receivers, so a low-bitrate MP3 round-trip is a defensible LiveATC proxy.

## 4. Randomized augmentation strategies

- **Uniform random sampling over a small transform menu works.** Google's MTR is exactly this — per-utterance random draw of (noise config, sample rate, codec) from fixed lists/ranges, "zero or more perturbations" per utterance. No ATC paper tunes distributions beyond uniform ranges.
- **RandAugment-style policies**: RandAugment reduced policy search to two hyperparameters (N transforms, magnitude M) in vision ([Cubuk et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3495724.3497287)); audio adaptations exist — [SapAugment (arXiv:2011.01156)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01156) learns *sample-adaptive* policies (harder augmentation for low-loss samples) and beat fixed SpecAugment policies on LibriSpeech; also automated DA for audio classification ([IEEE/ACM TASLP 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TASLP.2024.3402049)). Evidence that learned policies beat well-chosen uniform ranges for *channel* simulation specifically is thin.
- **Domain randomization** ([Tobin et al., IROS 2017](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/IROS.2017.8202133)) supplies the theoretical argument: with enough variability in simulation parameters, "the real world may appear to the model as just another variation." The audio analog is exactly the unseen-codec generalization above. A vision middle path is *learning* the sensor-effect augmentation distribution ([Sensor Transfer, arXiv:1809.06256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.06256)).

## 5. Procedural vs learned channel simulation — does it close the gap?

**No direct, controlled ATC-domain head-to-head exists**; evidence is piecemeal:
- Procedural sim demonstrably closes most of the *acoustic* gap cheaply: Bagat's three-step AAS bought 37% relative WER on TTS data; codec/noise MTR nearly equalized clean-trained and degraded-test performance.
- Learned channel/device models beat naive baselines in their niches: **MicAugment** ([ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2010.09658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09658)) identifies a mic/channel transform from seconds of target audio and "significantly increases model robustness to microphone variability" as augmentation; **multi-discriminator CycleGAN** domain adaptation improved noisy-speech ASR without parallel data ([Hosseini-Asl et al., ICASSP 2018, arXiv:1804.00522](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00522)); CycleGAN adaptation helped low-resource speaker recognition ([arXiv:1910.11909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.11909)), and CycleGAN augmentation combined with SpecAugment gave 4.3% WER reduction on CHiME-6 (reported via [SSL CycleGAN speech-enhancement literature](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364242387_Speech_Enhancement_Using_Augmented_SSL_CycleGAN)).
- The residual gap after procedural sim in ATC appears dominated by **accent, speaking rate, and phraseology**, not channel: in Bagat's results, voice/accent conversion (learned from real ATC audio) beat TTS+AAS, and combining them was best. This supports a hybrid design (procedural channel + learned voice/domain component), but that combination's superiority over procedural-only is **unvalidated in print**.

## Practical takeaways — concrete parameter ranges

1. **Band-limit every sample**: bandpass ~**300–2500 Hz** nominal (ICAO), randomized corners (HP 200–400 Hz, LP 2300–3400 Hz) to cover receiver variation; 200 Hz HP + 8 kHz resample alone gave Bagat 37% relative WER gain.
2. **Sample-rate round-trip**: decimate to 8 kHz (occasionally 4–6 kHz for aliasing character) and upsample back; apply with p≈0.5 (Google's mixed-bandwidth setting).
3. **Additive noise at SNR ~0–30 dB** (Google MTR), consistent with ATCO2's observed 0–40 dB with a >0 dB intelligibility floor; skew toward the clean end ("majority of data is clean").
4. **Nonlinearity**: tanh distortion plus occasional hard clipping (audiomentations ClippingDistortion) to mimic overmodulated AM; no published parameter ranges — tune against LiveATC references.
5. **Codec round-trip**: MP3/AAC at **{23, 32, 64, 128} kbps** uniform plus "no codec"; expect transfer to unseen codecs (~20% relative WER protection at MP3 23 kbps), so exact LiveATC bitrate matching is non-critical.
6. **Apply "zero or more" effects per utterance with independent random draws** (MTR pattern); leave a fraction fully clean; per domain-randomization logic, over-wide ranges are safer than over-narrow.
7. **Squelch tails, PTT clicks, heterodynes, AGC pumping**: no published parameters — implement physically (AGC-following noise floor, carrier-difference sine whistles from hundreds of Hz to ~2 kHz, click transients at segment boundaries) and validate perceptually; measure statistics from ATCO2/LiveATC if rigor is needed.
8. **Watch model size**: channel augmentation reliably helps small/medium models; one ATC study found it neutral-to-harmful for Whisper Large v3 Turbo — A/B test before committing.
9. **Budget expectation**: channel simulation closes the acoustic gap but not the accent/phraseology gap; the latter needs learned components (VC/accent conversion) or real in-domain data — consistent with the hybrid TTS+DSP+learned-channel plan.
