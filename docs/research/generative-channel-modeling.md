# Literature Survey: Generative Models for Audio Channel/Noise Domain Adaptation (state of the art, mid-2026)

Scope: learning the channel+noise characteristics of ~1,000 real ATC VHF clips (2–10 s) and applying that "learned channel" to clean TTS output — i.e., the inverse of speech enhancement.

---

## 1. CycleGAN / unpaired translation approaches

**Spectrogram-domain CycleGAN for speech domain adaptation** was established by the Multi-Discriminator CycleGAN ([Hosseini-Asl et al., ICASSP 2018, Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/a-multi-discriminator-cyclegan-for-unsupervised-non-parallel-speech-domain-adaptation/)), which adapts spectrograms between source and target speech domains with multiple frequency-band discriminators, explicitly motivated by ASR domain adaptation without parallel data.

**MaskCycleGAN-VC** ([Kaneko et al., ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2102.12841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12841)) is the strongest CycleGAN-family model for non-parallel mel-spectrogram conversion. It adds a "filling in frames" self-supervised auxiliary task and outperformed CycleGAN-VC2/VC3 in subjective naturalness/similarity. Critically for our data budget, the CycleGAN-VC line is routinely trained on **minutes** of speech per domain (VCC-scale data), so ~1k clips (~1–2 h) is comfortably above its usual regime.

**Most directly on-point:** *Study of GANs for Noisy Speech Simulation from Clean Speech* ([Maben et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.12460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12460)) tackles exactly our task — clean→noisy translation covering **UHF/VHF radio, additive stationary/non-stationary noise, and codec distortion** (RATS, TIMIT-Cabin, TIMIT-Helicopter). They propose SpeechAttentionGAN, SimuGAN, and MaskCycleGAN-Augment, trained on datasets of only **~3 minutes**, and report Multi-Scale Spectral Loss improvements over baseline of **55.8% (RATS), 28.9% (TIMIT-Cabin), 22.8% (TIMIT-Helicopter)**. Two cautions from this line of work: (a) neural vocoders (MelGAN) trained on clean speech **fail on noisy spectrograms**, producing unintelligible output — so operate waveform-side or use a robust vocoder/Griffin-Lim; (b) cycle-consistency forces near-deterministic mappings, so stochastic noise diversity is limited (the model tends to learn an "average" channel).

**ASR-augmentation evidence:** CycleGAN-converted speech as augmentation gave **3.39–3.82% relative WER reduction** for children's ASR ([EUSIPCO 2021](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2021/pdfs/0000511.pdf)); CycleGANs have also been used for low-resource channel adaptation in speaker recognition ([arXiv:1910.11909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.11909)). Known weaknesses: training instability and mode collapse ([CycleDiffusion, MDPI 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/20/9595)).

## 2. Diffusion / score-based approaches

**SGMSE+** ([Richter et al., IEEE/ACM TASLP 2023, arXiv:2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.05830v3), [code](https://github.com/sp-uhh/sgmse)) defines a diffusion process in the complex-STFT domain whose **forward SDE interpolates from clean to noisy speech** — i.e., the forward process *is* a channel/degradation model; the enhancement direction is its reverse. **StoRM** ([Lemercier et al., TASLP 2023, arXiv:2212.11851](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11851)) adds a predictive first stage to cut steps and artifacts. **UNIVERSE** ([Serrà et al. 2022, arXiv:2206.03065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03065)) trains score-based enhancement against a **hand-built degradation chain of ~55 distortions** (band-limiting, codecs, clipping, reverb, transmission artifacts) — the inverse framing you'd invert for degradation modeling. A broad survey is [Lemercier et al., IEEE SPM 2024, arXiv:2402.09821](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09821v3).

**Degradation-direction diffusion** (clean→noisy generation) is much less published than the enhancement direction — no paper found doing conditional diffusion specifically to *stamp* a learned radio channel onto clean speech at 1k-clip scale (uncertainty: this appears to be a genuine gap, not a search failure). Closest: noise-model-guided diffusion ([GDiffuSE, arXiv:2510.04157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04157)) and diffusion-generated synthetic audio for SSL pretraining (Gao et al. 2024, cited in [LREC 2026 augmentation study](https://lrec.elra.info/lrec2026-main-442)).

**Inference cost is largely solved** via flow matching / consistency-style training: [FlowSE, Interspeech 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19476), **Shortcut Flow Matching** ([arXiv:2509.21522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21522)) reaching **RTF 0.013 single-step** with quality comparable to a 60-NFE diffusion baseline, [MeanFlowSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14858v3) (one-step, no distillation), and [FINALLY, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05920v3). These are enhancement-direction but the machinery transfers to degradation-direction training.

**Small-data risk:** diffusion models conditioned on the clean utterance only need to model the *residual* (channel+noise), which reduces data demands, but 1k short clips is below typical training scales in the cited work; expect need for heavy augmentation and small models (uncertainty flagged — no direct empirical evidence at this scale).

## 3. Alternatives

- **DDSP-style differentiable signal chains — the standout for our setting: MicAugment** ([Gfeller, Li et al., Google/ETH, ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2010.09658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09658)) identifies a microphone/channel from **a few seconds of target audio** using a strongly inductive-biased differentiable pipeline (learned filter + nonlinearity + additive noise), then applies it as augmentation, significantly improving downstream robustness. This is essentially the "fit a parametric channel by gradient descent" answer and is proven in the one-shot regime.
- **Noise extraction + additive resynthesis:** VAD-based extraction of noise segments from target-domain recordings, then SNR-controlled mixing — validated as **persoDA** ([arXiv:2501.09113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09113)) and target-noise-injection variants; noise-aware simulation with stochastic perturbation for domain-adaptive enhancement ([arXiv:2409.01545](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01545)). Cheap, robust, zero mode-collapse risk; with ~1k clips you can harvest a real noise bank plus per-clip SNR/EQ statistics.
- **Environment-aware TTS:** condition synthesis directly on environment embeddings — [Environment-Aware TTS (arXiv:2110.03887)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.03887), [DAIEN-TTS (arXiv:2509.14684)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14684) (disentangled speaker vs environment prompts), [UmbraTTS flow-matching (arXiv:2506.09874)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09874), ImmersiveTTS. Attractive long-term, but couples TTS and channel; harder to retrofit onto an existing TTS stack.
- **Channel embeddings / SSL features:** SSL representations demonstrably encode channel/recording-condition information (probing analyses, [arXiv:2501.05310](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05310v3)); radio-channel-robust speaker verification work simulates WBFM/NBFM channels over VoxCeleb and attributes **~50% of degradation to bandwidth limitation alone** ([arXiv:2406.10956](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10956)). Useful for building channel probes and conditioning vectors rather than as a standalone generator.
- **Neural codec for radio:** [RADE (arXiv:2505.06671)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06671) — neural codec over HF radio; tangential but confirms learned models capture radio-channel structure.

## 4. Small-data regime (~1k clips)

- CycleGAN-family results at **~3 minutes** of training data ([arXiv:2305.12460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12460)) and VC results on minutes-scale corpora indicate GANs are viable at our scale; the binding constraint is **diversity** (many stations/frequencies/handsets in 1k clips → a deterministic translator averages them) rather than raw quantity.
- Discriminator-side augmentation transfers from vision: **StyleGAN2-ADA** ([Karras et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://github.com/NVlabs/stylegan2-ada-pytorch)) and **DiffAugment** ([Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.10738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10738)) show GANs matching large-data quality with a few thousand samples by augmenting both real and fake discriminator inputs with differentiable transforms — directly applicable to spectrogram discriminators (SpecAugment-style masks, gain/SNR jitter).
- MicAugment operates **one-shot**; fitting one parametric channel per real clip yields ~1k channel presets to sample from — turning the small dataset into a distribution over channels rather than a single averaged channel.
- Diffusion at this scale is the least-proven option (see §2 uncertainty).

## 5. Evaluation of domain match

- **FAD** ([Kilgour et al., Interspeech 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335830031)) with modern embeddings: VGGish correlates poorly with perception in several domains (<0.1 Spearman for environmental sound vs >0.5 for PANNs-based embeddings); **embedding choice and reference-set quality dominate** — see *Adapting FAD for Generative Music Evaluation* ([Gui et al., ICASSP 2024, arXiv:2311.01616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01616)), which also handles **sample-size bias by extrapolation** (relevant: our reference set is only 1k clips).
- **Kernel Audio Distance (KAD)** ([overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/kernel-audio-distance-kad)): MMD-based, distribution-free, unbiased, better small-sample behavior than FAD — arguably the right default with a 1k-clip reference set.
- **Embedding-space distances** over CLAP/WavLM: C-FAD variants and codec-embedding distances ([arXiv:2509.18823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18823)); WavLM layers encode channel info ([arXiv:2501.05310](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05310v3)) → build a **channel probe**: train a small real-vs-synthetic classifier on embeddings; chance-level accuracy = domain match.
- **Downstream WER as the end metric:** the ATC literature validates this directly — fine-tuning Whisper with synthetic ATC audio (alone or mixed with real) significantly improves ATCO2 WER ([Bagat et al., Interspeech 2026, arXiv:2606.21340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21340)); WhisperATC reports **24.03%→16.74% WER on ATCO2** ([Research Square pipeline paper](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-8970162/v1) reports out-of-box Whisper-small at 63.32% vs 22.69% fine-tuned on real ATCO2 — numbers vary widely with model size/subset, treat cross-paper comparisons cautiously).
- MOS-nets (DNSMOS/UTMOS) are useful inverted: synthetic-degraded audio should match the *distribution* of MOS scores of real clips, not maximize them.

## 6. ATC/radio-specific applications

- **Bagat et al., Interspeech 2026** ([arXiv:2606.21340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21340)): the closest full pipeline — TTS + VC + accent conversion + acoustic property simulation; synthetic-only fine-tuning already beats real-only baselines on ATCO2 with Whisper.
- **ROSE** ([arXiv:2312.06118](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06118v2)): key negative result — GAN/diffusion *enhancement* of ATC audio improves perceptual metrics but **degrades ASR accuracy** (generation distorts ASR-relevant features). Implication: train ASR on realistically degraded audio rather than enhancing at inference; also a warning that generative channel models must preserve linguistic content (verify with ASR-consistency checks on synthetic output).
- **DeCGAN** ([Algorithms 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/a18050245)), **ATCSpeechNet** ([arXiv:2102.08535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.08535)), GWU ICNS 2026 "DeepFake ATC comms" TTS simulator, and maritime VHF analogues ([ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666822X24000121)) round out the domain.

## Comparison table

| Criterion | CycleGAN (spectrogram) | Conditional diffusion / flow | DSP-hybrid (MicAugment-style + noise bank) | Env-aware TTS / channel embedding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data efficiency (~1k clips) | Good — proven at minutes-scale; needs ADA/DiffAugment | Weakest evidence at this scale (unproven) | **Excellent — one-shot per clip** | Moderate; needs diverse env prompts |
| Training cost | Low–moderate (hours on a 5080) | High (days; distillation adds a stage) | **Minimal** (per-clip fits, embarrassingly parallel) | High (retrain/fine-tune TTS) |
| Inference cost | Low (1 fwd pass) | Low *after* flow-matching/1-step training, else high | **Negligible (DSP graph)** | Same as TTS |
| Controllability | Poor (entangled, no knobs) | Moderate (guidance, conditioning) | **Excellent (interpretable params: SNR, EQ, clipping, codec)** | Good (prompt/embedding) |
| Diversity | Poor–moderate (deterministic mapping averages channels) | **Excellent (stochastic sampling)** | Good (sample from ~1k fitted presets + noise bank) | Moderate |
| Risk | Mode collapse; vocoder failure on noisy spectra; artifacts that mislead ASR | Overfitting at 1k clips; cost; hallucinated content | Residual realism gap (what DSP can't parameterize) | Coupling to TTS stack; least mature |

## Recommendation

1. **Backbone: DSP-hybrid.** Fit a MicAugment-style differentiable channel (band-limit ~300–3400 Hz, EQ, AGC/compression, memoryless nonlinearity/clipping, codec) **per real clip** by gradient descent, and harvest a **real noise bank** via VAD from the same clips (persoDA-style). Sample (channel preset, noise segment, SNR) triples per synthetic utterance. This matches the existing pipeline philosophy, is proven in the one-/few-shot regime, and gives interpretable knobs plus real diversity.
2. **Residual realism: lightweight MaskCycleGAN-style translator** trained clean-DSP-degraded → real, with DiffAugment/ADA-style discriminator augmentation and waveform-side operation (avoid clean-trained neural vocoders on noisy spectra). It only has to learn what the DSP chain misses, which shrinks both the modeling burden and the artifact risk. Gate its inclusion on the metrics below.
3. **Defer diffusion** to a second phase: conditional clean→noisy diffusion with flow-matching/1-step inference is attractive for diversity but unproven at 1k clips and costlier; revisit if the GAN residual underperforms.
4. **Evaluation harness (build first):** KAD + FAD on CLAP and WavLM embeddings against the 1k-clip reference (with sample-size extrapolation), a real-vs-synthetic **channel probe** on WavLM embeddings (target: near-chance), DNSMOS *distribution* matching, ASR-consistency of degraded synthetic audio (per ROSE's warning), and the end metric: **ΔWER of Whisper fine-tuned on synthetic vs real ATC test sets** (ATCO2-style protocol per Bagat et al.).

Main uncertainties: no published conditional-diffusion channel-stamping at 1k-clip scale (gap, not disproof); cross-paper ATCO2 WER numbers are not directly comparable; KAD/FAD embedding choice for narrowband radio speech is untested — validate with the channel probe before trusting either.
